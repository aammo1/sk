# Emotion-first strategy reference

## Reasoning lens for steps 7–15
Start with a lived moment, not a list of product specifications.
Ask what the friction may mean to this audience, not what everyone secretly feels.
Use “may”, “could” and “hypothesis” for emotional interpretation, even after angle approval.
Map situation → friction → meaning → desire/identity → transformation → angle
→ emotion → copy technique → creative treatment → hook → ad.
Features explain the mechanism supporting the transformation; they are not the whole pitch.
Explore belonging, status, autonomy, relief or calm only where the context supports them.
Emotional intensity comes from recognizable detail, not humiliation or invented danger.
Present the chain across separately approved stages, not as one bulk approval request.

## Example 1: organizer — reclaim the moment before leaving
Premise: the owner confirms separate compartments and a visible layout; accept these facts.
Audience hypothesis: someone repeatedly searching for small daily essentials before leaving home.
Situation: shoes on, ready to leave, one hand checking the same drawer again for the keys.
Practical frustration: small items disappear among other objects and interrupt departure.
Possible emotional meaning: “I want to start my day on my terms, not already feeling scattered.”
Desire/identity hypothesis: Order supports feeling prepared; Tranquility supports a calmer start.
Desired transformation: from repeated searching to a familiar place to reach for essentials.
Angle: “Give the start of your day a little breathing room.”
Emotion: `40 - comfort`, framed as scattered → more settled, rather than generic cleanliness.
Copy approach: a short recognizable story; compartments explain the practical support.
Creative direction: an honest everyday scene, then items returned to their separate places.
Hook execution: a searching hand revisits the drawer; map to a live chip only after selection.
Illustrative ad: “Keys. Earbuds. That one little thing. Give your daily essentials a place
to come back to, with separate compartments and a layout you can see at a glance.”
Avoid “never lose anything again”, invented time savings and assumptions about personal worth.

## Example 2: hand warmer — stay present in a cold routine
Premise: the owner confirms rechargeable operation and adjustable heat; do not demand proof again.
Audience hypothesis: people whose ordinary outdoor waiting routines are made uncomfortable by cold hands.
Situation: waiting outside during a familiar morning routine, shifting attention to cold fingers.
Practical frustration: discomfort competes with the conversation or surroundings.
Possible emotional meaning: “I'd like to feel comfortable enough to stay in this moment.”
Desire hypothesis: Tranquility may fit; Social contact fits only if connection is an approved angle.
Desired transformation: from attention pulled toward discomfort to a more comfortable pause.
Angle: “A little warmth for the moments you're already in.”
Emotion: `40 - comfort`; no manufactured health fear or assumptions about loneliness.
Copy approach: situation-led story; rechargeable operation and adjustable heat support the benefit.
Creative direction: hands holding the warmer in a believable waiting scene, without fake testimony.
Hook execution: a close detail of hands in the cold, then a quiet return to the wider moment.
Illustrative ad: “Cold hands can steal your attention. Bring a little warmth to your everyday
pause, with adjustable heat and rechargeable operation. Explore the hand warmer.”
Do not invent runtime, temperature, medical benefits, weather resistance or guaranteed relief.
