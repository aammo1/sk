# Emotion-first strategy reference

## Reasoning lens

Start with a lived moment, not a list of product specifications. Ask what
the friction may mean to this audience, not what everyone secretly feels.
Use "may", "could" and "hypothesis" for emotional interpretation, even
after the direction is approved.

Walk the chain: situation → friction → meaning → desire/identity →
transformation → angle → emotion → copy → creative → hook → ad.

Features explain the mechanism supporting the transformation; they are not
the whole pitch. Explore relief, belonging, status, control, comfort and
identity only where the context supports them. Emotional intensity comes
from recognizable detail, not humiliation or invented danger.

## Hypothesis discipline

Label which parts are observed, owner-confirmed, researched quotes or
hypothesis when saving into the note. Approval adopts the hypothesis for
the campaign; it does not prove it. If later results test the angle,
record them as campaign evidence without converting the underlying
psychology into fact.

## Mapping to live catalogs

Save the approved direction using exact labels from the live catalogs:
human desires (e.g. `Tranquility`, `Order`, `Social contact`), emotions
(e.g. `40 - comfort`), copy techniques (e.g. `story`, `questions`),
creative techniques and hooks. Match labels to the narrative, not the
product type. Detailed reasoning goes in the body; property values stay
exact catalog labels.

## Worked example 1: organizer — calmer departures

- Premise: owner confirms separate compartments and a visible layout.
- Audience hypothesis: someone repeatedly searching for small daily
  essentials before leaving home.
- Situation: shoes on, ready to leave, checking the same drawer again for
  keys and phone.
- Friction → meaning: small items keep disappearing among other objects —
  the person may feel scattered before the day even starts.
- Desire/identity: `Order` supports feeling prepared; `Tranquility`
  supports a calmer start.
- Transformation: from repeated searching to a familiar place for daily
  essentials.
- Angle (customer-facing): "Un endroit fixe pour vos essentiels, et des
  départs plus calmes."
- Emotion: `40 - comfort`, framed as scattered → settled, not generic
  tidiness.
- Illustrative copy: "Clés. Écouteurs. Chargeur. Chaque chose a sa place.
  Fini de fouiller le même tiroir avant de sortir — des compartiments
  séparés pour retrouver vos essentiels en un instant."
- CTA when confirmed: "Commander maintenant — Paiement à la livraison."
- Avoid: "ne perdez plus jamais rien", invented time savings, assumptions
  about personal worth.

## Worked example 2: hand warmer — stay present waiting outside

- Premise: owner confirms rechargeable operation and adjustable heat.
- Audience hypothesis: people whose ordinary outdoor waiting routine is
  made uncomfortable by cold hands.
- Situation: waiting outside during a familiar routine, attention pulled
  toward cold fingers.
- Meaning: "I'd like to feel comfortable enough to stay in this moment."
- Desire: `Tranquility`; `Social contact` only if connection to others is
  part of the approved angle.
- Transformation: from attention pulled toward discomfort to a more
  comfortable pause.
- Angle: "Un peu de chaleur pour les moments que vous vivez déjà."
- Illustrative copy: "Le froid peut voler votre attention. Retrouvez le
  confort de vos pauses, avec une chaleur réglable et une batterie
  rechargeable."
- Avoid: invented runtime, temperatures, medical claims, weather
  resistance or guaranteed relief.

## Honest intensity

An emotional shift such as scattered → calm or cold → present is enough.
Fear, jealousy and shock are choices, not defaults; they apply only when
the danger is truthful, proportionate and relevant to the audience. Never
frame ordinary moments as emergencies to force a response.
