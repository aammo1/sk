# Creative strategy inspiration library

## Purpose

Use this reference to expand the range and quality of ad concepts after the
product facts, audience situation and strategic direction are understood. It
distills patterns observed in the public Notion and Foreplay creative boards
listed under [Source material](#source-material).

This is an inspiration system, not a mandatory framework:

- Do not copy an observed ad, brand voice, layout or script.
- Do not force every product into every format or desire.
- Do not treat inclusion in a swipe file as evidence that an ad performed.
- Do not let a clever format replace product truth, audience relevance or a
  coherent emotional direction.
- Do not override the approved Morocco COD workflow, package shape, factual
  constraints or persistence rules.
- Adapt principles to the product, Moroccan context, customer language,
  available assets and approved strategy.
- Prefer one strong, product-relevant idea over a collage of fashionable ad
  devices.

The library is most useful when a concept is strategically sound but visually
ordinary, when three concepts feel too similar, or when a proven direction
needs fresh executions without changing its core promise.

## The three-layer model

Keep three decisions separate. Many weak briefs call all three an "angle",
which makes later testing impossible to interpret.

### 1. Desire: why the customer cares

The desire is the human outcome beneath the product benefit. Examples include
calm, belonging, control, attraction, competence, convenience or confidence.
It is a campaign hypothesis, not a psychological fact about every customer.

### 2. Messaging strategy: what changes the customer's mind

The strategy is the persuasive route. It may teach a mechanism, demonstrate a
process, tell a personal story, answer an objection, expose a contrast or make
an avoided problem discussable.

### 3. Creative format: how the argument appears in the feed

The format is the visible delivery system: UGC video, before/after static,
comparison chart, headline card, carousel, founder video, annotated product
image or another treatment.

Example:

> Desire: feel composed before leaving home.  
> Strategy: demonstrate how a fixed place reduces repeated searching.  
> Format: split-screen before/after static or a 20-second routine video.

Changing the desire tests motivation. Changing the strategy tests persuasion.
Changing the format tests delivery. Do not change all three in a variation
that is supposed to produce a useful learning.

## How to use this reference

Use a short selection process rather than browsing every option mechanically:

1. Restate the approved audience moment and transformation.
2. Identify the one belief, uncertainty or objection the ad must move.
3. Identify what truthful proof is available: visible demonstration,
   confirmed feature, price, construction detail, review, research quote or
   no external proof yet.
4. Select one primary messaging strategy that can move that belief.
5. Select one format that makes the available proof easiest to understand on
   a phone.
6. Add one hook that earns attention without misrepresenting the message.
7. Remove any visual or sentence that does not serve the single promise.

Use the library divergently first, then converge:

- Diverge by considering two or three plausible format-strategy pairings.
- Converge on the pairing with the strongest product fit, clearest proof,
  best mobile readability and lowest trust cost.
- Keep the approved core promise consistent enough that the product page can
  continue the same argument.

## What the audited ads reveal

### Patterns shared by stronger ads

- The first frame has a recognizable visual grammar: comparison, handwritten
  note, oversized number, customer comment, annotated object or visible
  transformation.
- The promise is specific. Time, quantity, audience, situation, mechanism or
  result replaces vague adjectives such as "amazing" and "premium."
- The product has a clear role in the outcome. It is not an arbitrary pack
  shot pasted under emotional copy.
- One primary idea dominates. Secondary benefits support it rather than
  competing with it.
- Proof is visible or concrete: a process, construction detail, side-by-side
  state, quote, calculation or confirmed fact.
- Copy and visual perform different jobs. The image demonstrates or creates
  context; the copy interprets its meaning.
- The message remains understandable without sound and before the viewer
  reads long primary text.
- The destination continues the promise instead of forcing the customer to
  rediscover what the product is after clicking.

### Patterns shared by weaker ads

- Generic praise such as "life-changing," "must-have" or "I'm obsessed"
  without a concrete use case.
- Too many arrows, benefits, logos, statistics or type styles.
- Article and comment screenshots that cannot be read on a phone.
- Founder stories that delay product relevance for too long.
- UGC scripts that sound memorized and contain more claims than a real person
  would naturally say.
- Fake regret, fake news, fake comments or faux independence that creates a
  short attention spike at the expense of trust.
- Before/after claims with inconsistent lighting, angle, styling or timing.
- Emotional language unrelated to the demonstrated product mechanism.
- Novel visual treatments that hide the product and offer.

### Important evidence limitation

The audited boards are curated examples. They contain duplicated campaigns,
uneven brand representation and no reliable spend, conversion, delivery or
profit data. Large boards often reflect one active advertiser rather than a
universally successful pattern. Use the observations to generate hypotheses,
then judge those hypotheses with Moroccan COD results: confirmed orders,
delivered orders, revenue and contribution margin.

## Creative format library

### Before and after

**Job:** Make a transformation immediately visible.

**Typical construction:** Split screen, stacked states, old/new labels, red
crosses versus green checks, or the product positioned between the problem
and improved state.

**Works best when:** The outcome is genuinely visible or can be represented
honestly through a routine, environment or state change.

**Useful proof:** Same setting, same angle, same lighting, confirmed number of
uses, or an observable process that caused the change.

**Risks:** Staged results, exaggerated medical or cosmetic implications,
unfair visual conditions and an unrealistic time horizon.

**Morocco COD application:** Show an ordinary local situation rather than an
abstract studio transformation. An organizer can show a cluttered departure
routine versus a visible fixed place; a cleaning tool can show one real
surface before and after use. Do not fabricate customer results.

### Bullet points

**Job:** Compress several reasons to buy into a skimmable value stack.

**Typical construction:** Product or person as anchor, three to five short
benefits, icons or checkmarks, and one dominant headline.

**Works best when:** Customers need several concrete reasons before the
product feels worth its price.

**Useful proof:** Each bullet maps to a confirmed feature, demonstrated use
or sourced claim.

**Risks:** Tiny type, repeated ideas, unsupported claims and a missing primary
benefit. More bullets do not automatically create more value.

**Morocco COD application:** Prefer three customer-language outcomes over a
technical inventory. If COD trust is relevant and confirmed, keep payment or
delivery reassurance visually separate from product benefits.

### Carousel

**Job:** Sequence a story, comparison, routine, collection or explanation.

**Typical construction:** A strong cover, one idea per card, visual continuity
and a final product/action card.

**Works best when:** The idea benefits from progressive disclosure and the
first card creates a real reason to continue.

**Useful proof:** Each slide answers one customer question or advances one
step in the same argument.

**Risks:** Weak cover, repetitive cards, unrelated gallery images and placing
the essential message only on later slides.

**Morocco COD application:** A useful sequence is situation → friction → how
the product helps → confirmed feature → COD action. Keep each card readable
without relying on the caption.

### Celebrity or recognizable authority

**Job:** Borrow attention, familiarity, expertise or cultural status.

**Typical construction:** Recognizable person in the opening frame,
endorsement, interview, collaboration or expert-led explanation.

**Works best when:** There is a real, authorized relationship and the person's
relevance to the product is obvious.

**Useful proof:** Genuine usage, permissioned quote, professional expertise or
documented collaboration.

**Risks:** Unauthorized likeness, invented endorsement, expensive production,
audience mismatch and the person overshadowing the product.

**Morocco COD application:** Never imply a Moroccan public figure, clinician,
creator or media outlet endorses the product without evidence and permission.
Real micro-creators with category fit may be more credible than fame alone.

### Feature and benefit point-out

**Job:** Make the product's functionality tangible.

**Typical construction:** Product centered in frame, arrows or leader lines
pointing to materials, compartments, controls or practical outcomes.

**Works best when:** Construction and functionality are important but not
obvious from an ordinary pack shot.

**Useful proof:** Real photographed details and confirmed specifications.

**Risks:** Product-page appearance rather than an ad, unreadable labels and
features that never connect to a customer outcome.

**Morocco COD application:** Annotate only the details that support the
approved promise. Translate technical facts into short, natural French or
Darija while preserving accuracy.

### Founder story

**Job:** Humanize why the product or store exists and create accountability.

**Typical construction:** Founder to camera, specific problem or observation,
discovery, product rationale and invitation.

**Works best when:** The founder has a genuine story, useful expertise or a
recognizable experience connected to the product.

**Useful proof:** Real chronology, behind-the-scenes decisions, sourcing or
demonstration.

**Risks:** Generic biography, delayed relevance, overproduced authenticity and
invented origin stories.

**Morocco COD application:** For resellers, do not fabricate an invention or
founder narrative. A truthful seller story can instead explain why this item
was selected, what practical detail stood out and who it may help.

### Headline-led static

**Job:** Test one proposition with maximum clarity.

**Typical construction:** Large headline in the upper visual hierarchy,
minimal supporting image and one product/CTA anchor.

**Works best when:** The idea can be understood in one short line and the
product image supports rather than repeats it.

**Useful proof:** A support line, visible feature or clear situation.

**Risks:** Generic copy, excessive cleverness and sparse layouts that look
unfinished rather than deliberate.

**Morocco COD application:** Use natural customer language, not literal
translations of English swipe-file headlines. Keep the headline short enough
to survive Meta crops and Arabic-script rendering where applicable.

### Negative marketing

**Job:** Interrupt expectations through criticism, warning, contradiction or
an apparent negative review.

**Typical construction:** "Why I regret...", "Do not buy this if...", common
mistake, old-solution criticism or a negative expectation that becomes a
positive product argument.

**Works best when:** The negative framing reveals a truthful tradeoff, calls
out a real category frustration or qualifies the audience honestly.

**Useful proof:** Demonstration, clear comparison or a specific explanation.

**Risks:** Clickbait, fake regret, competitor disparagement, wrong audience
expectations and erosion of brand trust.

**Morocco COD application:** Prefer truthful qualification such as "Pas pour
vous si vous cherchez..." over manufactured one-star-review theater. Never
invent a customer complaint to smuggle in praise.

### Novel or borrowed-interface formats

**Job:** Escape ad blindness by resembling useful or entertaining non-ad
content.

**Typical construction:** Calendar, quiz, chat, checklist, meme, Notes app,
search result, diagnostic table, map or routine planner.

**Works best when:** The borrowed format naturally explains the product.

**Useful proof:** The interface contains real, useful information rather than
decorative imitation.

**Risks:** Novelty without clarity, deceptive impersonation, dated memes and
an obscured product role.

**Morocco COD application:** Adapt interfaces and humor to actual Moroccan
mobile behavior and language. Do not imitate banks, government portals,
delivery companies or messaging identities in a deceptive way.

### News

**Job:** Make information feel urgent, important or newly discovered.

**Typical construction:** Broadcast lower third, presenter, bulletin, report,
timestamp or article reveal.

**Works best when:** There is genuine news, a real public discussion, a
verifiable product development or a clearly fictionalized entertainment
device that cannot be mistaken for reporting.

**Useful proof:** Named source, date, direct citation and transparent context.

**Risks:** False authority, fake urgency, financial or medical misinformation
and severe landing-page trust loss.

**Morocco COD application:** Avoid fabricated television identities, subsidy
announcements, government claims or local-media branding. An educational
"update" should be clearly branded as the seller's explanation.

### Post-it note or handwriting

**Job:** Create a low-production pattern interrupt and qualify the audience.

**Typical construction:** One handwritten message on a physical note, product
package or ordinary surface, followed by demonstration or explanation.

**Works best when:** The sentence is short, legible and genuinely specific to
the intended customer.

**Useful proof:** The content after the note fulfills the promise made by the
first frame.

**Risks:** Saturation, illegible writing and a bait-style opening unrelated to
the sales message.

**Morocco COD application:** Use natural handwriting and locally familiar
phrasing only if the script is readable. The note can introduce one lived
moment or objection rather than shouting a generic sale.

### Press

**Job:** Borrow third-party authority and reduce perceived purchase risk.

**Typical construction:** Publication excerpt, award, named expert quote or
editorial recommendation paired with the product.

**Works best when:** The coverage is real, relevant and legible.

**Useful proof:** Exact outlet, quote, date and source link.

**Risks:** Invented logos, vague "as seen in" strips, unreadable screenshots
and using a broad article as if it endorsed the exact product.

**Morocco COD application:** If there is no real press, do not simulate it.
Use factual demonstration or owner-confirmed features instead.

### Statistics

**Job:** Turn a complex benefit into one precise, memorable fact.

**Typical construction:** One oversized number, clean composition, short
interpretation and a source or qualification.

**Works best when:** The number is directly applicable, meaningful and easy
to understand.

**Useful proof:** Verifiable source, sample, timeframe and product relevance.

**Risks:** Precision theater, omitted baselines, study laundering and dense
charts that appear scientific without being comprehensible.

**Morocco COD application:** Do not import percentages from a competitor or
similar product. If no verified product statistic exists, use confirmed
counts such as number of compartments only when factually accurate.

### Social proof

**Job:** Show that relevant other people chose, discussed or trusted the
product.

**Typical construction:** Real comment, customer post, adoption count,
community discussion or recognizable user endorsement.

**Works best when:** The source is authentic, permissioned, legible and
specific about the experience.

**Useful proof:** Original record, customer permission and accurate wording.

**Risks:** Fabricated comments, unverified celebrity attribution, cropped
context and generic praise.

**Morocco COD application:** With no reviews, replace social proof with a
factual demonstration. COD skepticism is not permission to invent orders,
ratings, WhatsApp messages or delivery testimonials.

### Testimonial

**Job:** Let a prior user's experience answer risk and relevance questions.

**Typical construction:** Quote, attribution, product image, customer photo,
stars or a first-person video narrative.

**Works best when:** It names the prior problem, use context, timeframe and
specific result without promising universal outcomes.

**Useful proof:** Genuine review and permission where required.

**Risks:** Generic "obsessed" language, atypical outcomes presented as normal,
fabrication and visual sameness.

**Morocco COD application:** Preserve the customer's actual language and do
not improve a quote into a stronger claim. If no testimonials exist, omit the
format.

### UGC

**Job:** Combine peer-like attention, explanation, demonstration and proof in
a native vertical video.

**Typical construction:** Everyday setting, direct-to-camera hook, product in
hand, close demonstration, personal interpretation and CTA.

**Works best when:** The creator has a believable relationship to the use
case and speaks naturally.

**Useful proof:** Product behavior on camera, specific routine and real
experience.

**Risks:** Scripted "authenticity," delayed product identification, excessive
claims and copying foreign creator mannerisms that feel culturally false.

**Morocco COD application:** Use familiar environments, rhythms and customer
language. Make the COD action clear without turning the whole video into a
price announcement. A seller demo can be more honest than pretending to be
an independent customer.

### Us versus them

**Job:** Simplify why a buyer should switch from an existing solution.

**Typical construction:** Two columns, checks and crosses, old/new method,
product/category comparison or cost/time tradeoff.

**Works best when:** Buyers understand the current alternative and the
difference is material.

**Useful proof:** Comparable criteria, confirmed specifications and fair
conditions.

**Risks:** False binary, misleading superiority, legal issues and increasing
competitor salience.

**Morocco COD application:** Comparing with "the usual method" may be safer
and clearer than naming a competitor, but claims must still be fair. Do not
invent competitor prices, materials or failure rates.

### Whitelisting or creator-handle delivery

**Job:** Transfer creator familiarity and audience trust to an ad.

**Typical construction:** Ad delivered from a creator identity with native
content, personal routine, demonstration or creator-specific offer.

**Works best when:** The creator genuinely fits the audience and the paid
relationship is properly disclosed.

**Useful proof:** Real usage and creator-specific experience.

**Risks:** Audience mismatch, hidden sponsorship, lost brand recognition and
creator copy overloaded with brand claims.

**Morocco COD application:** Treat whitelisting as distribution plus trust,
not an idea by itself. The creative still needs a desire, argument and proof.

## Desire and identity lenses

Use these lenses to widen strategic thinking, not to diagnose customers.
Select only desires supported by the product, audience situation and research.

### Acceptance

**Underlying hope:** "This can fit me, include me or help me feel understood."

Signals include accessible use, inclusive fit, accommodation, belonging and
social approval. Separate acceptance from status: acceptance is horizontal
belonging, while status is vertical distinction.

### Curiosity

**Underlying hope:** "I can discover something useful or surprising."

Questions, quizzes, hidden mechanisms and incomplete information activate
curiosity. The reveal must reward the click; do not create a mystery the
product page cannot satisfy.

### Family

**Underlying hope:** "I can care for, help or stay present with people who
matter to me."

Ground the angle in a concrete role or shared moment. Avoid guilt, invented
danger or assuming every buyer has the same family structure.

### Independence

**Underlying hope:** "I can choose, act or manage this without unnecessary
dependence."

Useful for convenience, flexible routines, self-service and mobility. Separate
independence from power: independence removes constraints; power directs
outcomes.

### Physical activity

**Underlying hope:** "I can move, perform, participate or recover with less
friction."

Center capability and participation when that is what the product supports.
Do not automatically reduce physical activity to thinness or appearance.

### Power and control

**Underlying hope:** "I can influence the result instead of feeling at the
mercy of the problem."

Control may mean visibility, preparation, informed choice or a manageable
routine. Avoid grandiose promises that the product cannot deliver.

### Romance and attraction

**Underlying hope:** "I can feel noticed, attractive or more connected to a
partner."

Use only when naturally relevant. Avoid humiliation, jealousy, sexual
inadequacy and rigid gender stereotypes.

### Social contact

**Underlying hope:** "I can participate, share or connect more easily."

Products may enable a shared activity, remove a participation barrier or act
as social currency. Separate contact from acceptance: contact enables the
interaction; acceptance resolves whether the person feels included.

### Social status

**Underlying hope:** "I can appear current, capable, tasteful or respected."

Status can be subtle: a composed home, professional presentation or informed
choice. Do not substitute borrowed logos and luxury signals for product value.

### Tranquility

**Underlying hope:** "I can reduce noise, effort, uncertainty or repeated
mental load."

Strong situations include preparation, sleep, organization and simplified
routines. Show what becomes calmer rather than using generic wellness imagery.

### Additional commercial desires

The source taxonomy is not complete. The lenses below are narrative
inspiration, not automatically valid vault labels. When one is useful, map it
to an exact approved live catalog label or keep it only in the written
reasoning; never invent a new property value. Consider these when supported:

- **Convenience:** fewer steps, easier access or less repeated effort.
- **Economy:** better use of money, lower waste or clear value.
- **Safety:** reduced exposure to a truthful, relevant risk.
- **Order:** visible structure, predictability and a known place or process.
- **Mastery:** confidence in performing a task correctly.
- **Comfort:** physical or emotional ease in a familiar situation.
- **Novelty:** enjoyment of a genuinely new experience or design.
- **Certainty:** clearer expectations about use, delivery, payment or result.

## Persuasive messaging strategies

### Knowledge sharing

Teach something that changes how the customer understands the problem. Useful
forms include a hidden mechanism, myth versus fact, checklist, decision aid,
routine or fair comparison.

Strong knowledge sharing answers "why should I believe this?" Weak knowledge
sharing merely decorates claims with scientific-looking language.

Use when the product needs category education, when a familiar problem is
misunderstood, or when useful information can earn attention before the sale.

### Tutorial

Show a repeatable process the viewer could perform. The product should make a
meaningful step easier, faster or more reliable.

A tutorial usually follows task → steps → visible result → next action. Hands,
close-ups, numbered overlays and untreated/treated comparisons help.

Use to reduce perceived difficulty, uncertainty, setup anxiety or misuse.

### Overcoming objections

Begin with a real reason a qualified customer may hesitate. Validate it,
explain the tradeoff, supply proof and reframe the decision.

Useful objections in COD include:

- "Will it look like the photo?"
- "Do I really need this?"
- "Is it difficult to use?"
- "Is the price justified?"
- "What happens after I submit the order?"
- "Can I pay when it arrives?" only when confirmed.

Do not introduce an obscure objection that most customers did not have.

### Personal history

Use chronology to create identification: prior situation → friction → failed
assumption or attempt → discovery → changed routine → invitation.

Personal history can be a customer, creator or seller story. Keep sponsored
or seller identity transparent and do not fictionalize vulnerability.

### Pet-context framing

Use pets as a realistic use condition, emotional cue or audience qualifier.
The strongest form is a relevant stress test: fur, mess, durability, storage
or calm shared space. A cute animal with no product connection is only bait.

### Taboo candor

Name an awkward but real problem plainly enough that the customer feels
recognized rather than shamed. Then normalize it, explain it and present a
proportionate solution.

Use humor carefully. Blunt language can improve memorability but may affect
Meta approval, audience comfort, age suitability and premium positioning.

### Founder, celebrity and negative framing as hybrids

Some source boards list founder stories, celebrity and negative marketing as
both formats and strategies. Treat them deliberately:

- Founder content is a format when the founder merely presents the ad; it is
  a strategy when origin and accountability are the reason to believe.
- Celebrity is a format when recognition stops the scroll; it is a strategy
  when relevant authority or identity transfer changes the decision.
- Negative framing is a hook when it only opens the ad; it is a strategy when
  a real category weakness becomes the central argument.

## Hooks and proof devices

### Hook families

- **Direct situation:** "Encore en train de chercher vos clés avant de
  sortir ?"
- **Visible problem:** Open on the friction before any explanation.
- **Unexpected result:** Show the outcome, then explain how it happened.
- **Specific question:** Ask something a clearly defined buyer recognizes.
- **Contrarian statement:** Challenge a common method only when the claim can
  be defended.
- **Customer words:** Use a real question or review with permission.
- **Number:** Lead with one meaningful confirmed quantity or sourced result.
- **Demonstration:** Begin the action immediately.
- **Objection:** State the hesitation directly.
- **Audience qualification:** "Pour celles et ceux qui..." without implying
  sensitive personal knowledge.
- **Story opening:** A specific moment, not "Let me tell you my story."
- **Comparison:** Put the old and new experience in the same frame.

### Proof hierarchy

Prefer the strongest proof actually available:

1. Direct visible demonstration under fair conditions.
2. Confirmed product construction, specification or operation.
3. Genuine customer experience with context and permission.
4. Directly applicable study, test or external source with qualification.
5. Fair calculation or comparison with disclosed assumptions.
6. Seller explanation clearly identified as seller explanation.
7. Unsupported assertion, which should be removed rather than styled as
   proof.

### Match hook to payoff

The body of the ad must satisfy the expectation created by the hook. A quiz
hook needs an answer, a comparison hook needs fair criteria, a statistic needs
context, and a dramatic problem needs a proportionate demonstrated solution.

## Combining the layers

Use these combinations as prompts for original thinking, not fixed recipes.

| Commercial job | Desire lens | Strategy | Format | Proof |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Make a new product understandable | Mastery or control | Knowledge sharing | Point-out static or carousel | Confirmed mechanism |
| Show immediate practical value | Convenience | Tutorial | UGC demonstration | Visible process |
| Reduce price hesitation | Economy or certainty | Objection handling | Comparison or calculation | Fair cost/value breakdown |
| Sell a visible change | Confidence or comfort | Demonstration/history | Before and after | Consistent conditions |
| Humanize an unfamiliar store | Acceptance or certainty | Seller history | Founder-style video | Transparent identity and rationale |
| Switch an old method | Control or convenience | Contrast | Us-versus-them | Comparable criteria |
| Make an awkward problem discussable | Comfort or confidence | Taboo candor | UGC or headline static | Explanation plus demonstration |
| Build trust without reviews | Certainty | Tutorial | Product demo | Real product behavior |
| Use real customer evidence | Acceptance or certainty | Testimonial | Quote static or UGC | Specific genuine review |
| Create calm around COD | Certainty | Objection handling | Simple process graphic | Confirmed order and payment steps |

Before accepting a combination, ask:

- Does the desire arise naturally from the customer's situation?
- Does the strategy move a real belief or merely decorate the product?
- Does the format make the proof clearer on mobile?
- Can every claim be traced to a confirmed fact or source?
- Does the concept preserve trust through the COD confirmation and delivery
  experience?

## Morocco COD adaptation

### Design for the complete order path

The ad does not complete the sale by itself. It earns a qualified click to a
product page, which earns an order submission, which must survive confirmation
and delivery. A creative that generates impulsive curiosity but mismatched
expectations can create unconfirmed orders, refusals and operational cost.

The ad should therefore make clear:

- What the product is.
- Who or what situation it is for.
- The primary practical or emotional outcome.
- The product's role in that outcome.
- The actual offer when confirmed.
- The next action.

### Localize situations, not just words

Translation alone does not create relevance. Adapt:

- The everyday environment and objects.
- Clothing and casting where people appear.
- The rhythm and directness of spoken language.
- Familiar buying questions and COD uncertainty.
- Mobile typography for French, Darija or Arabic script.
- The degree of humor, directness and formality appropriate to the product.

Avoid reducing "Moroccan" to decorative clichés. A believable kitchen,
bedroom, entryway, work bag, school morning or delivery interaction is often
more locally credible than flags, mosaics or generic traditional styling.

### French and Darija considerations

- Write customer language naturally rather than translating an English hook
  word for word.
- Keep French concise and conversational.
- When using Darija, choose Latin or Arabic script deliberately and maintain
  consistent direction and typography.
- Do not mix languages merely to appear local; code-switch where the intended
  audience would naturally do so.
- Read every line at feed size. Arabic-script diacritics, joins and rendering
  errors can make otherwise correct copy unusable.

### COD trust without generic badge clutter

Use only confirmed trust information. Relevant signals may include payment on
delivery, expected confirmation call, delivery coverage, expected timing,
exchange policy or product inspection terms. Do not display all of them on
every ad.

Choose the one reassurance most relevant to the concept. Keep operational
trust separate from the emotional headline so it supports rather than replaces
the product argument.

### Reseller honesty

Many COD stores sell products they did not invent. Do not manufacture a
founder-invention story, clinical development story or exclusive mechanism.
The seller can truthfully explain product selection, demonstrate operation,
compare practical methods or answer ordering concerns.

## Static-ad construction

Build a static ad in this order:

1. **Single communication objective:** What should the viewer understand or
   believe after one glance?
2. **Visual evidence:** What image, comparison, detail or action can carry
   that meaning?
3. **Headline:** Interpret the visual with one clear promise.
4. **Support line:** Add the mechanism or most important confirmed detail.
5. **Product anchor:** Make the item recognizable and preserve fidelity.
6. **CTA:** State the next action without invented urgency.
7. **Trust cue:** Add one only when relevant and confirmed.

### Visual hierarchy

At feed size, the eye should encounter:

1. Hook or visual result.
2. Product and primary meaning.
3. Supporting evidence.
4. CTA or trust cue.

If everything is the same size, nothing leads. If the headline requires more
than a moment to decode, simplify it. If labels require zooming, remove them.

### Purposeful variation

Variation A and B should share the same core promise. Change one meaningful
element, such as:

- Direct-problem hook versus desired-outcome hook.
- Demonstration image versus annotated product image.
- Customer question versus declarative headline.
- Product close-up versus lived-situation composition.

Do not change hook, audience, promise, layout, offer and proof simultaneously
and then call the result a controlled variation.

## Video-ad construction

### Recommended 20–30 second architecture

**0–3 seconds: Hook**

Show or state the situation, result, question or objection. The opening must
work with sound off.

**3–8 seconds: Recognition**

Make the everyday friction concrete. Avoid inflated drama.

**8–16 seconds: Product and mechanism**

Show what the product is and what it does. Use close-ups, hands and a visible
sequence where possible.

**16–23 seconds: Proof or objection resolution**

Demonstrate the confirmed feature, compare fairly, quote real evidence or
clarify how ordering works.

**23–30 seconds: Outcome and action**

Return to the desired state and give one clear CTA. Include COD reassurance
only when confirmed and useful.

### Shot principles

- Reveal the product early enough to qualify the viewer.
- Change visual information regularly without frantic editing.
- Use captions that summarize, not transcribe every filler word.
- Film the mechanism close enough to understand.
- Keep backgrounds believable and uncluttered.
- Capture separate hook shots so the same body can test multiple openings.
- Let sound support the visual; do not make comprehension depend on audio.

### UGC voice

A natural creator usually communicates one experience and a few reasons, not
an entire product page. Short sentences, specific observations and visible
actions feel more credible than a dense recital of features.

If no real customer or creator is available, write a transparent seller demo
rather than presenting an actor as an independent satisfied buyer.

## Testing and diagnosis

### Test in layers

Use this sequence when planning future campaign tests. A new desire or
emotional direction is a strategy revision: present it as a hypothesis, obtain
approval and update the saved direction before producing replacement
deliverables. It is not an unapproved variation inside the current package.

1. Across approved strategy iterations, test two or three plausible desires
   while holding product, offer and broad format stable.
2. For the strongest desire, test persuasive strategies such as demonstration,
   objection handling and knowledge sharing.
3. For the strongest strategy, test formats such as static comparison, product
   callout and UGC video.
4. Test hook variations within the strongest combination.
5. Test offer changes only with confirmed economics and explicit approval.

### Interpret metrics by stage

- **Weak thumb-stop or 3-second view:** First frame, hook or visual grammar
  may not earn attention.
- **Strong view rate, weak hold:** Hook may promise something the body does not
  deliver, or the message becomes slow or confusing.
- **Strong engagement, weak outbound click:** Entertainment or education may
  not connect clearly to product desire and next action.
- **Strong click-through, weak page engagement:** Ad-page message mismatch,
  accidental clicks or unclear product expectations may be present.
- **Strong submitted orders, weak confirmation:** Offer comprehension, buyer
  intent, price surprise or confirmation process may be the issue.
- **Strong confirmation, weak delivery:** Delivery timing, expectation match,
  address quality or buyer commitment may be the issue.
- **Cheap clicks without delivered margin:** The creative is not commercially
  successful for the COD funnel.

Do not infer causality from one metric or a small sample. Record what changed,
what remained constant and what downstream data is still missing.

## Failure modes and safeguards

### Format-first thinking

Failure: "Make a UGC ad" without defining why the audience should care.

Correction: Specify desire, belief to move, proof and then format.

### Swipe-file copying

Failure: Recreating another brand's composition, script and hook with a new
pack shot.

Correction: Abstract the persuasion principle and rebuild it from the actual
product situation, evidence and local customer language.

### Classification obsession

Failure: Spending effort deciding whether an ad is social proof, testimonial,
UGC or whitelisting when it may be all four.

Correction: Classify only enough to clarify what is being tested.

### Unsupported proof theater

Failure: Adding stars, percentages, press logos, comments or medical styling
because those devices appear persuasive.

Correction: Use only evidence that exists. When evidence is absent, demonstrate
the confirmed product behavior.

### Emotional overreach

Failure: Turning an ordinary inconvenience into shame, danger, jealousy or
social inadequacy.

Correction: Build intensity through a recognizable moment and a meaningful,
proportionate transformation.

### Mobile unreadability

Failure: Treating a 4:5 ad like a desktop brochure.

Correction: One headline, one support point, one visual argument and a clear
product. Move detail to the product page.

### Foreign creative mimicry

Failure: Copying American creator speech, settings, prices, institutions or
cultural jokes into a Moroccan campaign.

Correction: Preserve the strategic principle while rebuilding the execution
around actual local language, context and buying behavior.

### False urgency

Failure: Invented stock warnings, countdowns or "today only" language.

Correction: Use real deadlines only. Product relevance and clear value should
carry the ad when urgency is not factual.

### Funnel blindness

Failure: Optimizing for CTR while creating low-intent COD orders.

Correction: Evaluate the ad through confirmation, delivery and margin, not the
platform click alone.

## Final inspiration check

Before including a concept in the package, verify:

- The audience moment is concrete.
- The emotional meaning remains a hypothesis.
- The desire is relevant rather than fashionable.
- The message moves one identifiable belief or objection.
- The selected format makes the argument clearer.
- The hook and payoff match.
- The product appears early and accurately.
- The proof exists and is represented fairly.
- The text is readable on a phone.
- The customer language sounds natural.
- The concept is adapted, not copied.
- The product page can continue the same promise.
- COD claims and operational terms are confirmed.
- No review, statistic, endorsement, scarcity or guarantee was invented.
- The variation changes one meaningful element.
