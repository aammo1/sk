# Morocco COD marketing

## Purpose and boundaries

- Guide a Moroccan cash-on-delivery marketer from one product input to an
  approved, production-ready ad package plus a product-page marketing brief.
- Use ad → product page → order form → order confirmation → delivery →
  payment as the default analytical model. Confirm whether confirmation is
  by call, message or another process before presenting it as a fact. Success
  means confirmed and delivered orders, not cheap clicks or form submissions.
  There is no email channel to build; the ad sells the click to the product
  page, and the page closes the order.
- Make emotional meaning the core strategy; verified features support how
  the product helps. Use the canonical strategy chain defined below.
- Approval adopts a campaign hypothesis; it does not turn interpretive
  psychology into fact. Keep "may", "could" and "hypothesis" language even
  after the user approves an angle.
- Welcome strong emotional persuasion while preserving honest brand trust.
  Do not invent capabilities, statistics, testimonials, guarantees, proven
  outcomes, reviews, certifications, deadlines or scarcity.
- Avoid stereotypes, unsupported sensitive inferences, and automatic fear,
  jealousy or shock framing. Emotional intensity comes from recognizable
  detail, not humiliation or invented danger.
- Accept explicit user-confirmed features and prices as facts; do not
  repeatedly demand proof.
- Work in `/home/tal7a/Work/MARKETING_VAULT`. Read
  [Vault and persistence](references/vault-persistence.md) before touching
  any note, template or table. The only override is an explicit evaluation
  prompt that supplies an isolated temporary vault; in that case, use only
  the supplied sandbox and never access the production vault.

## Defaults (overridable per product or niche)

- Market: Morocco, COD funnel.
- Customer-facing copy: French. The user may choose Darija, Arabic script,
  English or another language per product; confirm during essentials.
- Strategy notes, explanations and conversation: English (or the user's
  language). Generation prompts are written in English with the exact
  approved customer-facing text embedded.
- Placement: Meta (Facebook and Instagram).
- Standard ad package: six static ads (three concepts × two purposeful variations),
  three video scripts when requested (one per concept), and one product-page
  marketing brief containing four gallery prompts. Change this scope only
  when the user explicitly approves a different production scope.

See [Ad package](references/ad-package.md) for formats and templates.
Use [Creative strategy inspiration](references/creative-strategy-inspiration.md)
when the approved direction needs richer hooks, messaging routes or visual
executions. Treat it as an optional idea library: adapt principles rather than
copying examples, and never let it override confirmed facts, the three
checkpoints, the package contract or the approved emotional direction.

## Canonical terms and chain

- **Desire:** why the customer may care.
- **Emotion:** the feeling connected to the situation or transformation.
- **Angle/promise:** the main customer-facing reason to buy.
- **Messaging strategy:** how the ad changes a belief or resolves an objection.
- **Format:** the visible delivery method.
- **Concept:** one combination of promise, strategy, proof and format.
- **Hook:** the opening attention device.
- **Variation:** a controlled change to one named element of a concept.

Use one chain throughout: situation → friction → possible meaning →
desire/identity → transformation → angle → emotion → copy → concept → hook →
ad. Refer to it as the **canonical strategy chain** instead of shortening or
renaming its steps.

Choose the mode before using any checkpoint:

- **Create:** a new product strategy from an image, URL or description.
- **Resume/revise:** continue an existing note in `01 Products`; ask resume

- **Review results:** bypass the three creation checkpoints and follow

For create, resume and revise, follow the checkpoints below. Results review

## Checkpoint 1 — Essentials and research

Purpose: build an accurate product and commercial picture, fill real gaps,
then move. Do not rehearse late-stage decisions here.

1. Accept a local image, image URL, product page URL or description.
   Inspect the actual image if tools support it; otherwise disclose the
   limitation and ask for the missing description. Never claim to have
   viewed an image you could not inspect.
2. Resolve note identity before writing. Match in order by an exact stored
   product ID or source URL, exact factual product identity, then exact note
   filename. Never resume or overwrite from fuzzy similarity. If zero or
   multiple plausible notes exist, show the proposed new path or candidates
   and ask the user. If a note exists, reread it and confirm resume versus
   revise. Preserve manual edits and legacy analysis.
3. Confirm the essentials: product identity, confirmed features, current
   price and offer terms, delivery and payment policies, available assets,
   and the customer-facing language. Always ask explicitly whether video ads
   are needed (yes or no) in this checkpoint. If yes, include the standard
   three video scripts; if no, omit them completely. Do not ask about other
   defaults unless needed; list applied defaults in the summary so the user
   can correct them.
4. Read [Research and offers](references/research-offers.md). Inspect supplied
   pages when URL fetching is available; do discovery research only when
   search is available. Do a short, focused pass: the
   product page (identification and claimed features), a few customer
   review or Q&A snippets, and relevant visible competitor ads or pages
   without inferring that visibility proves performance. Look for customer
   wording, buying objections and competing
   promises. Record sources and quotes, keeping evidence separate from
   interpretation in `## Research notes`. Keep the pass light: two or
   three focused searches, not an exhaustive study. If browsing is
   unavailable, proceed with clearly labelled hypotheses.
5. Ask one short batch of consequential questions only: missing facts the
   strategy would otherwise build on wrongly (identity, price, key
   features, delivery terms). Skip questions already answered or
   recoverable from the supplied context.
6. Present one approval payload: factual product identity, confirmed facts,
   offer and operational terms, language, video choice, applied defaults,
   research findings, unresolved non-blocking questions, proposed note path,
   cover action and initial property selections. Ask explicitly for approval
   to initialize or update that note. Do not write or advance while a
   consequential fact remains unresolved.
7. On explicit approval, initialize the note using the blank-state schema in
   the persistence reference (or continue the existing note), save the setup
   and research findings, update `## Workflow progress`, then move to
   Checkpoint 2.

Output of this checkpoint: an approval summary followed, only after approval,
by an initialized or updated note.

## Checkpoint 2 — Strategy direction

Purpose: one recommended, commercially strong direction the user reacts to
as a whole, instead of walking through catalog screens.

Present one package covering:

- **Customer-facing marketing name:** recommend one attractive, natural name
  in the approved customer language, with a short rationale. French is the
  default only when French is selected. Keep it distinct from the factual
  catalog identity and use it only after approval.
- **Audience:** a concrete group in a recognizable situation, plus the
  practical problem the product solves for them.
- **Emotional meaning:** what the friction may mean to them and the
  desire/identity hypothesis behind it (see
  [Emotional strategy](references/emotional-strategy.md)).
- **Transformation:** the intended shift, e.g. scattered → calm, without
  invented danger or dramatic fear.
- **Angle:** the campaign promise in plain customer words.
- **Feature support:** the confirmed features that make the promise
  believable; benefits lead, features follow.
- **Objections:** the two or three strongest buying objections, including
  COD concerns such as trust, delivery or need.
- **Offer framing:** the confirmed price and terms as the baseline. Suggest
  an offer change only when there is a clear commercial reason, and get
  approval before it enters any customer-facing text. Discount or bundle
  math requires the relevant cost information first (see
   [Research and offers](references/research-offers.md)).
- **Vault mapping:** show the proposed exact live desire, emotion and copy
  technique labels concisely. This mapping is part of direction approval;
  creative-technique and hook labels belong to Checkpoint 3.

Ask for one approval of the direction as a whole. The user may correct any
single element without re-approving the rest; iterate only on what they
dispute.

Save only the approved exact live labels; never write invented labels. Save
the direction immediately after approval, following the persistence contract.

Fear, jealousy or shock may be considered only when the danger is truthful,
proportionate and relevant; they are never defaults.

## Checkpoint 3 — Marketing package

Purpose: turn the approved direction into ready-to-use deliverables.

Produce the standard package below. It may be delivered in three compact
blocks—statics, requested videos, then page brief/gallery prompts—to prevent
truncation and review fatigue, but all blocks share one final package approval.

1. **Six static ads** — three concepts (default families: the lived problem
   and its solution; the main benefit demonstrated; the strongest objection
   answered), each with two variations. Variation A is the baseline;
   Variation B differs from A in exactly one named meaningful element while
   everything else remains materially constant.
   Give every variation its own Meta primary text, headline and description,
   clearly labelled with the same concept and variation name as its prompt.
2. **Three video scripts, only when requested at Checkpoint 1** — one per
   concept, 9:16, 20–30 seconds, hook in the first 2–3 seconds. Give each
   requested video its own Meta primary text, headline and description. If
   the user answered no, do not generate video concepts or scripts.
3. **Product-page marketing brief** — the message, evidence and CTA the
   page must carry, for the user's separate design skill; see
   [Product-page handoff](references/page-handoff.md). It contains four
   product-gallery prompts with distinct purposes: clean hero, realistic use,
   feature close-up and aspirational lifestyle. Show complementary attractive
   product positions rather than repeating one composition.

Each ad and requested video must trace the canonical strategy chain and
include exact customer-facing text in the approved language. Gallery prompts
omit rendered text unless the user explicitly requests it. Keep Meta primary
text, headline and description separate from on-image text. Follow
[Ad package](references/ad-package.md) for the full per-format template.
When concepts risk feeling repetitive or generic, consult
[Creative strategy inspiration](references/creative-strategy-inspiration.md)
to explore product-relevant combinations of desire, persuasive strategy,
format, hook and proof. Select only what strengthens the approved direction;
do not turn the reference into a checklist or copy its examples literally.

Present the package for review. Refine only on the user's request; on
approval, save it per the persistence contract and mark the checkpoints
done.

A production prompt or script authorizes no image/video generation, purchase
or ad launch by itself. Those actions need a separate request.

## Edge cases

- Missing delivery, return or guarantee terms: omit those promises and flag
  them for confirmation rather than inventing them.
- No reviews or testimonials: use a factual product demonstration instead;
  never fabricate social proof.
- Research unavailable: proceed with clearly labelled audience hypotheses.
- Unclear product identity or a key feature the angle depends on: ask one
  focused question before building on it.
- User changes an approved decision: preserve prior text, mark affected
  checkpoints `needs-review` in the authoritative `## Workflow progress`
  section, and get replacements approved.
