# Mobile design and persuasion library

The goal is beautiful, polished, persuasive sections suited to the product, brand, and strategy. These self-contained families and recipes are optional creative starting points, not mandatory templates or exact-match targets. Adapt, mix, or invent layouts and styles, including a coherent direction outside this library. Read only useful entries; preserve truthful claims, authentic evidence, accessibility, and mobile quality.

## 1. Visual families

**Monochromatic infographic** — Use one dominant brand/product color, a pale tint, a dark readable text color, and white panels. Large images alternate with concise explanations and icon summaries. The repeated graphic language makes a long explanation easy to scan. Suitable for practical benefit and use instructions.

**High-contrast direct response** — Bold heading bands, strong benefit statements, demonstrations, comparisons, and clear CTA blocks. Reserve the strongest contrast for the most important message and action rather than making every element equally loud. Suitable for an ad's specific problem-solving angle.

**Soft lifestyle/editorial** — Cream, blush, peach, or pastel fields; large lifestyle images; gentle curves; generous but purposeful spacing. Pair aspiration with a concrete explanation. Keep body text dark for readable contrast.

**Dark technical showcase** — Charcoal panels, white headings, bright restrained accents, macro detail images, floating cutouts, and light/dark transitions. Use for a construction or performance highlight, not as a reason to restyle the surrounding store.

**Annotated technical catalog** — Labeled components, close-ups, dimensions, exploded views, compatibility, and setup steps. Give each visual an explanatory job. This family resolves practical uncertainty before purchase.

**Playful handmade collage** — Curved arrows, brushstroke headings, offset frames, organic shapes, dots, and layered cutouts. Select one or two motifs and preserve a simple reading path. Useful for warm, informal storytelling.

## 2. Reusable section recipes

### A. Outcome spotlight
**Question:** What becomes easier or better for me?
**Mobile composition:** short benefit headline → large in-use photograph → brief explanation → two or three supporting facts. Optionally let a cutout overlap the edge of a color block, while keeping the text in normal flow.
**Persuasion:** outcome-first framing and mental simulation; the visitor pictures ownership.
**Assets:** relevant lifestyle/use image and supported benefit.
**Reuse:** make it a mid-page feature spotlight, not a second full hero. Desktop can pair image and text with a sensible image maximum width.

### B. Problem recognition gallery
**Question:** Is this relevant to my situation?
**Mobile composition:** a question headline, followed by 2×2 or 2×3 photographic tiles. Use circular crops or softly rounded squares with very short labels. End with a calm transition into the solution.
**Persuasion:** self-recognition; several visitors can identify with different everyday frustrations.
**Assets:** specific relevant situations, not unrelated discomfort imagery.
**Reuse:** 3–6 situations maximum, with label readability taking priority over column count. Do not exaggerate consequences to create fear.

### C. Problem → solution bridge
**Question:** Is there a better way to handle this?
**Mobile composition:** concise problem/question panel → contrasting reassurance band → large demonstration → one practical explanation.
**Persuasion:** Problem–Agitation–Solution, with proportionate agitation and immediate relief.
**Assets:** supported capability and a visual showing the solution.
**Reuse:** one clear problem is often enough. A strong bridge can be one section instead of three repetitive ones.

### D. Icon benefit summary
**Question:** What are the key advantages?
**Mobile composition:** 2×2 or 2×3 grid inside a pale/white panel; consistent icons; short benefit labels. Three columns are suitable only for short labels that remain readable.
**Persuasion:** scanning and information chunking.
**Assets:** 3–6 distinct factual benefits. Use the repository's existing icon set.
**Reuse:** translate technical features into outcomes. This is a summary, not the default layout for every section.

### E. Alternating photographic benefit rows
**Question:** How will each capability help me?
**Mobile composition:** 2–4 rows alternating image/text sides; roughly balanced visual blocks, short headings, one short explanation. Preserve two columns only while readable; stack longer rows. The checkerboard variants use a saturated text panel beside a flush photo.
**Persuasion:** feature-to-benefit translation with visual rhythm.
**Assets:** a distinct relevant photo for each claim.
**Reuse:** vary the message rather than restating the same benefit. Keep reading order sensible in RTL and for screen readers.

### F. Numbered reasons to choose
**Question:** Why choose this option?
**Mobile composition:** oversized numeral or number badge, clear benefit heading, short detail, and related crop; repeat with a consistent row pattern.
**Persuasion:** a finite, organized set of reasons feels easier to evaluate.
**Assets:** 3–5 genuinely distinct advantages.
**Reuse:** number reasons, not fake process stages. A number alone is not visual evidence.

### G. Central image with satellite benefits
**Question:** What does this one solution combine?
**Mobile composition:** central cutout with 3–4 short labels/icons around it, or cutout above a compact benefit row when width is insufficient. Use a restrained circle/blob behind the object.
**Persuasion:** all-in-one association and coherent grouping.
**Assets:** clean product image and short supported benefits.
**Reuse:** avoid tiny satellite paragraphs and crossing arrows. Desktop may keep the radial arrangement; mobile legibility comes first.

### H. Photographic benefit strips
**Question:** What does each benefit look like in use?
**Mobile composition:** 3–4 horizontal strips with a circular photo overlapping one edge, plus a bold short heading and one supporting sentence. Alternate edges only when it improves rhythm.
**Persuasion:** combines quick icon-like scanning with specific photographic information.
**Assets:** real close-up or in-use crops.
**Reuse:** ensure the overlapping circle has space and never covers text. Use local positioning rather than page-wide offsets.

### I. Annotated detail image
**Question:** Which details make the difference?
**Mobile composition:** large uncluttered image with 3–5 labeled callouts. For small screens, numbered dots can correspond to labels below rather than microscopic text around the object.
**Persuasion:** makes a claim visually specific and reduces abstract explanation.
**Assets:** correct component positions and supportable descriptions.
**Reuse:** use short labels and no crossed connector lines. Native text/SVG labels are preferable to unreadable embedded text.

### J. Macro construction gallery
**Question:** What will I actually receive, and how is it made?
**Mobile composition:** one dominant detail image and two smaller supporting crops, or stacked alternating close-ups with labels. A consistent border/background unifies the gallery.
**Persuasion:** helps replace physical inspection and supports quality claims.
**Assets:** accurate close-ups of construction, texture, controls, or connections.
**Reuse:** each crop should answer a purchase question. Do not include more angles just to lengthen the page.

### K. Exploded view and disassembly
**Question:** Is it understandable and easy to maintain?
**Mobile composition:** vertically separated parts with clear labels; short heading above, maintenance benefit below. Keep the component stack tall enough to read but not unnecessarily screen-filling.
**Persuasion:** lowers perceived complexity by revealing component relationships.
**Assets:** an accurate existing exploded image or a faithful diagram grounded in known construction.
**Reuse:** do not invent internal components. If no such asset exists, use real sequential disassembly photos.

### L. Mechanism or cutaway explanation
**Question:** How does it work?
**Mobile composition:** headline → explanatory diagram/cutaway → 2–4 labeled stages or short paragraphs. Use arrows only for meaningful flow.
**Persuasion:** gives a reason to believe through understandable explanation.
**Assets:** accurate mechanism information; label conceptual illustrations where appropriate.
**Reuse:** distinguish explanation from independent proof of results. Keep technical vocabulary subordinate to customer understanding.

### M. Before-and-after comparison
**Question:** What observable difference should I evaluate?
**Mobile composition:** equally sized paired images with explicit labels, an unobtrusive divider, and brief context underneath. A 50/50 pair works only if each image remains informative.
**Persuasion:** makes change concrete and quickly legible.
**Assets:** genuine comparable states, relevant conditions, and timing where applicable.
**Reuse:** a divider or play-like graphic does not justify a slider or video. Use a static pair by default; interactive controls need a real purpose and accessible implementation.

### N. Result or task progression
**Question:** What does the process look like over stages?
**Mobile composition:** three labeled stages connected by directional cues; stack vertically if details would be too small in a row.
**Persuasion:** communicates sequence rather than a magical instant transformation.
**Assets:** accurate stages, with real time/context if claims concern results.
**Reuse:** numbered task stages and timed result stages are different. Do not assign invented durations.

### O. Demonstration / visual experiment
**Question:** Can I see it doing the task?
**Mobile composition:** outcome heading → large demonstration image, sequence, or actual video → concise explanation of conditions and benefit. Two stills connected by a clear arrow can show a simple experiment.
**Persuasion:** observation is more tangible than adjectives such as “powerful” or “effective.”
**Assets:** real demonstration media. A still image is not a video; do not add a fake play control.
**Reuse:** use controls/poster/captions for an actual video, avoid sound autoplay, and respect page performance.

### P. Three- or four-step quick start
**Question:** Can I use it successfully?
**Mobile composition:** section title → numbered image/instruction pairs. Options include vertical photo rows, a readable 2×2 step grid, or alternating checkerboard panels. Each instruction starts with an action verb.
**Persuasion:** reduces anticipated effort and uncertainty.
**Assets:** actual instructions and relevant photos/icons.
**Reuse:** one meaningful action per step; include essential setup. Do not shorten the process by hiding necessary steps.

### Q. Maintenance reassurance
**Question:** Will ownership become another chore?
**Mobile composition:** cleaning/care photo, reassuring factual heading, 2–3 short instructions or an actual maintenance interval.
**Persuasion:** addresses ongoing effort, not just purchase excitement.
**Assets:** real care requirements and useful demonstration.
**Reuse:** do not promise “maintenance-free” unless supported. Show replacement needs if material to the claim.

### R. Portability and scale proof
**Question:** Will it fit my routine, storage, or travel needs?
**Mobile composition:** large hand/bag/pocket photo, optional measurement arrows, short benefit headline, dimension/weight caption if known.
**Persuasion:** replaces vague compactness claims with a familiar reference.
**Assets:** accurate scale imagery and actual measurements.
**Reuse:** do not change image proportions to make an object seem smaller. Desktop should constrain the photograph rather than enlarge it to unrealistic visual scale.

### S. Use-case or occasion gallery
**Question:** Where would I use this?
**Mobile composition:** 2×2 grid or a dominant scene with small supporting circular crops. Short captions identify distinct contexts.
**Persuasion:** relevance, versatility, mental simulation, and occasionally gift framing.
**Assets:** supported use situations; realistic lifestyle photos.
**Reuse:** avoid suggesting universal suitability from unrelated pictures. Select contexts aligned with the supplied strategy.

### T. Compatibility and fit reassurance
**Question:** Will it work with what I have?
**Mobile composition:** clear heading, short compatibility list or labeled device/size/connection grid, and a practical fit diagram if useful.
**Persuasion:** removes a concrete purchase blocker.
**Assets:** exact compatibility information; actual dimensions or connection details.
**Reuse:** include meaningful exceptions. Existing selectors remain the source of purchasable options; do not add competing option state.

### U. Ingredient/material spotlight
**Question:** What is it made of and why does that matter?
**Mobile composition:** large component/material image beside or above a short role explanation; use alternating rows for multiple components. Natural props can support the palette.
**Persuasion:** links composition to practical benefits.
**Assets:** actual ingredients/materials and supportable properties.
**Reuse:** avoid implying an ingredient is present because decorative stock imagery looks attractive.

### V. Customer experience cards
**Question:** What have real buyers experienced?
**Mobile composition:** 1–3 stacked review cards, authentic quote, name/context, optional rating and customer photo. Variants include overlapping circular portraits, quote marks, name/rating footer bands, or compact 2-column short reviews.
**Persuasion:** specific social proof and similarity to the buyer.
**Assets:** genuine reviews and permitted imagery. Customer-use photos can be more useful than portraits.
**Reuse:** no fake five-star aggregates or verification badges. Preserve material meaning when shortening a quote. Do not copy template testimonials or reviews about a different product.

### W. Transaction reassurance / guarantee / FAQ
**Question:** What happens after I order, and what if there is a problem?
**Mobile composition:** 3–4 compact policy icons or visible question-and-answer panels; a real guarantee can get its own restrained callout. Adjacent purchase link only if helpful.
**Persuasion:** risk reduction through understandable terms.
**Assets:** actual delivery, payment, support, and return information.
**Reuse:** use current policies, not template “free delivery,” “48 hours,” or guarantee durations. Accordion behavior is optional; use it only when it helps navigation and remains accessible.

### X. Offer, bundle, or value panel
**Question:** Is this worth the cost, and what is included?
**Mobile composition:** concise value explanation; optional factual savings comparison; current supported offer/quantity choices; purchase link. Visual choices include a single badge, side-by-side quantity panels, or a clean contrasting price block.
**Persuasion:** anchoring, cost-of-alternative framing, legitimate offer stacking, and choice simplification.
**Assets:** accurate current pricing and real included value. Derive variant-dependent amounts from existing state or omit redundant prices.
**Reuse:** do not introduce new discounts/bundles, catalog mutations, scarcity, or deadlines merely to fill the design. Keep commercial choices in the existing buying flow.

### Y. Included-items layout
**Question:** What exactly arrives in the order?
**Mobile composition:** organized flat-lay or packaging image with labels, quantities, and a short explanation. Clearly distinguish included and optional items.
**Persuasion:** completeness and reduced ambiguity.
**Assets:** actual package contents and imagery.
**Reuse:** useful below setup or compatibility sections; do not make decorative background props look included.

### Z. Final action bridge
**Question:** What should I do next?
**Mobile composition:** one brief outcome reminder, optional concise reassurance, a contrasting action linking to the current purchase block.
**Persuasion:** connects conviction to an easy next step.
**Assets:** actual purchase destination, not a new checkout.
**Reuse:** one bridge may be enough for a short addition. Labels should describe the real action; do not create a fake order submission button that only scrolls.

## 3. Supporting visual techniques

Apply only those that help the chosen composition:
- **Hierarchy:** large benefit heading, smaller supporting title, readable body. Do not make all text bold/centered; use emphasis intentionally.
- **Color-block bands and pills:** mark a new idea. Noninteractive titles should not be mistaken for action buttons.
- **Marker underlines / brushstroke backplates:** emphasize a short headline; keep rough edges away from letterforms.
- **Circular masks:** compact benefit thumbnails, symptom galleries, reviewer images. Avoid cropping essential detail.
- **Offset frames / overlaps:** visually connect image and section. Keep overlap local and leave enough text clearance.
- **Pedestals / tabletop staging / soft shadows:** isolate the object and add polish. Use actual product assets and restrained depth.
- **Gradients / fade-to-background images:** ease transitions; check contrast across every part of a gradient.
- **Waves / diagonal edges / organic blobs:** create section rhythm. Use SVG/CSS decoration rather than giant image backgrounds when appropriate.
- **Dots / halftone / textures / sparkles:** add atmosphere behind or around focal content, never over body copy.
- **Hand-drawn arrows:** lead between related elements; every arrow should have a meaningful destination.
- **Motion rings / directional arrows:** explain rotation, flexibility, assembly, or flow without pretending a still is interactive.
- **Human attention cues:** a gesture or gaze can lead toward a message. A model photo is illustrative, not a testimonial or professional endorsement.
- **Environmental staging:** connect the promise to a real setting; choose it for relevance, not generic attractiveness.
- **Quote marks / star treatments / trust seals:** use only appropriate real evidence. Icons alone do not establish verification or certification.
- **Repeated visual grammar:** keep radii, photo treatments, spacing, icon weight, and heading styles coherent across the addition.

## 4. Copy and offer techniques

**Outcome-first headlines:** state what changes for the customer. Support the promise in the section.
**Category + benefit:** useful when the function is unfamiliar; in a mid-page section, avoid repeating the full product introduction unnecessarily.
**Direct address and question openings:** use familiar customer language and recognizable situations.
**Feature → effect → benefit:** explain why a capability matters, not just what it is.
**Future pacing:** show a believable improved routine through words and imagery.
**Care/family motivation:** use relevant shared scenarios; do not infer suitability for all ages from a family photograph.
**Alternative-cost framing:** compare actual recurring costs or use a qualitative convenience argument when figures are absent.
**Skepticism handling:** acknowledge prior disappointment and provide a concrete difference rather than another unsupported promise.
**Curiosity:** open a specific information gap and immediately answer it with a mechanism/detail.
**Specificity:** genuine numbers, dimensions, and conditions make a message more useful. Unsupported precision reduces credibility.
**Effort reduction:** back “easy,” “quick,” or “simple” with visible steps or actual time requirements.
**Local familiarity:** use the page's language, currency formatting, genuine local review context, and existing order-confirmation process.
**Repetition:** repeat the core idea with new evidence, not identical paragraphs.
**Urgency/scarcity:** use only real offer conditions; decorative countdowns and fabricated stock are not part of this skill.

## 5. Choosing and combining patterns

Use one coherent visual direction and usually 1–3 useful sections. A listed family or recipe may help, but a mixed or entirely new composition is equally valid. Combine complementary functions: demonstration + quick start; construction detail + care reassurance; use-case gallery + compatibility; supported comparison + customer experience.

Avoid stacking three recipes that all say the same thing. Keep action color, heading language, image style, and spacing consistent. A good phone section communicates its main point during scanning, then rewards closer reading with practical detail.

Do not flatten the section into an image, redesign the storefront, replace the purchase UI, or optimize desktop at the expense of mobile. Express the chosen visual direction at phone size while remaining real, functional, responsive web content.
